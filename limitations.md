# Limitations

## Data limitations

1. **Synthetic data.** The Meridian Supply Chain Dataset is generated by its publisher for teaching. All entities (Meridian Components, CARR-1…4, WH-1…4, customers) are fictional. Patterns come from the generator, not from real carrier behaviour. For example, on-time and damage rates are nearly identical across carriers by construction. Findings show the *method* and must not be read as claims about real carriers.

2. **No penalty, claim or reshipment data.** True cost beyond freight is **modelled**: late penalty at 2% of revenue, damage write-off at 25% of units, reshipment by Air. Absolute true-cost figures change with these assumptions. The *ranking* of Sea vs Rail holds until the penalty rate exceeds 35.8% of revenue, but the size of the hidden costs is assumption-driven.

3. **`on_time_flag` conflicts with the dates.** For 2,224 of 4,500 shipments (49%), the flag disagrees with whether `delivery_date − ship_date` exceeded the carrier's quoted days. Actual transit days are spread evenly from 1 to 9 for every carrier, whatever its mode (Sea is quoted 14 days, Air 1 day). Consequences:
   * On-time / late metrics use the source flag (metric of record).
   * Transit-time and "variance vs quoted" figures are reported but **not used to rank carriers or select lanes**.
   * Average-delay figures (lane analysis) describe the dates and should not be read as SLA performance.

4. **Damage is a shipment-level flag.** The share of units damaged is unknown, hence the 25% write-off assumption.

5. **No shipment urgency, service level or customer delivery window.** The Air→Rail opportunity assumes the moved volume can tolerate slower transit. This must be validated.

6. **Quoted transit days are carrier-level, not lane-level.** `carrier.avg_transit_days` is one number per carrier, so "quoted days" do not vary by lane.

7. **Freight cost is given per line.** It is used as billed. The implied rate per kg varies between shipments (0.8× to 1.3× the contract rate), and the reason is not documented.

## Analytical limitations

8. **Small carrier–lane samples.** The 80 carrier × lane cells hold 29 to 112 shipments each, so lane-level rates for one carrier have wide confidence intervals. The "three most problematic lanes" are partly selected by chance (regression to the mean is likely).

9. **Expected-value scenario costing.** The strategy and mode-shift projections assume each carrier's historical rates (freight per kg, late %, damage %) would apply to the moved volume, and that carriers do not reprice when volume changes.

10. **Static capacity.** The analysis does not test whether Rail has capacity for extra volume, or whether Air volume discounts would be lost.

11. **"Total landed cost" proxy.** The 12% target is measured against network true logistics cost ($24.65M). Including product cost would make the denominator larger, and the target even harder to reach.

12. **Composite weights are a judgement.** The 50/30/20 weighting is editable in Excel (`Cost_Model!C71:C73`), and different weights change the overall ranking.

## Validation limitations

13. Excel formulas were evaluated with an independent open-source engine (`formulas`), not with Microsoft Excel itself. All 37,041 formulas evaluated without error. The cached results differ from SQL by at most $0.10 in network totals, because of how that engine rounds exact half-cent values. Excel recalculates in full when the file is opened (`fullCalcOnLoad`).

14. The SQL script was executed on PostgreSQL 16.2. Other dialects (MySQL, SQL Server) would need syntax changes: `\copy`, `date − date`, `STDDEV_SAMP`, `GREATEST`.
